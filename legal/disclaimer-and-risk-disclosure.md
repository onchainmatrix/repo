## 5. Digital-Asset Market Risk

Digital assets can experience extreme price volatility.

Market value can decline rapidly and can fall to zero.

There is no guarantee of an active market, sufficient trading volume, market depth, stable pricing, exchange or DEX liquidity, a listing on any platform, continued availability of any trading venue, or the ability to sell an asset at a desired price or time.

Market structure can also be affected by leverage, liquidations, manipulation, concentration, hacks, outages, regulatory action, macroeconomic events, or loss of market confidence.

## 6. ONMX Risk

ONMX is a digital asset deployed on BNB Chain.

The fixed maximum supply is **1,000,000,000 ONMX**.

Holding or acquiring ONMX does not guarantee appreciation, liquidity, exchange listing, a particular market price, protocol revenue, treasury growth, buybacks, burns, stablecoin distributions, governance rights beyond functionality actually implemented, or delivery of any future feature.

The legal and economic rights associated with ONMX are determined by applicable smart contracts, current protocol documentation, and transaction-specific terms.

Maximum supply, total supply, locked supply, treasury-controlled supply, and circulating supply are different concepts. Market-data providers can apply their own circulating-supply methodologies.

## 7. Token-Sale Risk

Participation in an ONMX token sale can involve additional risks, including price volatility after acquisition, absence of immediate liquidity, vesting or lock restrictions, inability to transfer tokens before applicable restrictions expire, changing market conditions between acquisition and unlock, technical issues affecting claims or settlement, regulatory or legal changes, and loss of value.

A seed, presale, or public-sale reference price is a transaction term or target reference and is not a guarantee of future market price.

## 8. Treasury Strategy Risk

The Onchain Matrix treasury is designed as productive protocol capital.

Treasury deployment can include stablecoins, major crypto assets, onchain yield strategies, tokenized RWAs, liquidity positions, and future approved credit activity.

Any of these activities can lose value.

Treasury risks include market risk, liquidity risk, concentration risk, strategy risk, counterparty risk, smart-contract risk, stablecoin risk, oracle risk, custody risk, settlement risk, protocol dependency risk, RWA issuer or redemption risk, and operational risk.

Diversification, limits, monitoring, and risk controls can reduce certain risks but cannot eliminate them.

Historical performance, pilot results, or prior treasury yield do not guarantee future results.

## 9. Yield and Revenue Risk

Yield and protocol revenue are variable.

Sources can include treasury strategies, credit activity, lending participation, origination and servicing fees, pool activity, marketplace activity, and other protocol services.

Revenue can be lower than expected, interrupted, negative after losses or costs, or unavailable.

Displayed APYs, modeled returns, projections, scenarios, or historical results can change and should not be treated as promised returns.

## 10. Buyback and Burn Risk

ONMX buybacks and burns are conditional mechanisms.

They can depend on available protocol surplus, treasury resilience, market liquidity, protocol policy, legal considerations, technical capability, and prevailing market conditions.

There is no guarantee that a buyback or burn will occur, occur at a particular time, involve a particular amount, or affect ONMX market value in any particular way.

## 11. Stablecoin Distribution Risk

Eligible stablecoin distributions can be used where permitted and implemented.

No distribution is guaranteed.

A distribution can depend on available surplus, treasury performance, protocol policy, legal and regulatory requirements, eligibility, technical infrastructure, applicable snapshot or claim mechanics, and operational considerations.

Stablecoins themselves can lose their peg, become illiquid, be frozen, face issuer risk, or experience technical or regulatory disruption.

## 12. Smart-Contract Risk

Smart contracts can contain errors, vulnerabilities, design flaws, or unforeseen interactions.

Audits, testing, monitoring, formal methods, code review, public verification, multisig controls, timelocks, or bug bounties can reduce risk but cannot prove that software is free from vulnerabilities.

Potential outcomes include loss of funds, locked funds, incorrect accounting, failed transactions, unauthorized actions, broken integrations, unexpected upgrades, or protocol interruption.

An audit is not a guarantee of security.

Only security reports actually published through official Onchain Matrix channels should be treated as completed Onchain Matrix audits or reviews.

## 13. Cybersecurity and Key Risk

Digital-asset systems can be targeted by phishing, malware, credential theft, wallet drainers, compromised frontends, DNS attacks, supply-chain attacks, social engineering, malicious browser extensions, key compromise, infrastructure attacks, and fraudulent impersonation.

Users are responsible for protecting their wallets, devices, credentials, and private keys.

## 14. Multi-Signature and Administrative Risk

Multi-signature custody and role separation can reduce single-key risk but do not eliminate operational or governance risk.

Risks can include signer compromise, signer unavailability, collusion, implementation errors, incorrect permissions, delayed emergency response, malicious or mistaken approvals, and vulnerabilities in underlying multisig or access-control infrastructure.

Public verification of addresses and permissions improves transparency but does not eliminate these risks.

## 15. Upgrade Risk

Upgradeable contracts can be modified through authorized upgrade mechanisms.

Even where upgrades require multi-party approval or a delay, changes can introduce new vulnerabilities, compatibility issues, changed assumptions, altered permissions, or unexpected economic effects.

Upgrade authority is itself a material risk and should be evaluated together with the applicable contract, controlling address, approval structure, and timelock or delay mechanism.

## 16. Oracle and Data Risk

Treasury valuation, collateral health, LTV calculations, liquidations, RWA pricing, reporting, and other protocol functions can depend on external or onchain data.

Data can be stale, incorrect, manipulated, unavailable, delayed, inconsistent across sources, or based on markets with insufficient liquidity.

Validation rules, deviation checks, staleness checks, market-depth controls, fallbacks, and circuit breakers can reduce risk but cannot guarantee accurate or executable pricing in every condition.

## 17. Collateralized Credit Risk

Collateralized credit introduces risks beyond ordinary token ownership.

These can include borrower default, collateral volatility, inadequate collateral, liquidation delays, liquidation shortfalls, insufficient market depth, oracle failure, maturity mismatch, concentration risk, legal enforceability risk, smart-contract failure, settlement risk, and counterparty or service-provider risk.

Overcollateralization does not guarantee full recovery.

Collateral can decline faster than it can be liquidated, and realized liquidation proceeds can differ from displayed market value.

## 18. Tokenized Debt Risk

Tokenized debt positions can involve credit risk, liquidity risk, maturity risk, pricing risk, transfer restrictions, settlement risk, legal or regulatory restrictions, smart-contract risk, and secondary-market risk.

Tokenization does not remove the economic or legal risks of the underlying credit exposure.

A transferable tokenized position can still be illiquid or trade at a significant discount.

## 19. Tokenized RWA Risk

Tokenized real-world assets can depend on offchain legal, operational, and financial structures.

Risks can include issuer risk, administrator risk, custody risk, redemption restrictions, settlement delays, market-hours mismatch, stale NAV or pricing, legal ownership issues, transfer restrictions, insolvency risk, regulatory change, and dependence on offchain service providers.

The presence of an onchain token does not eliminate these risks.

## 20. Stablecoin Risk

Stablecoins can experience loss of peg, issuer or reserve risk, liquidity shortages, redemption restrictions, blacklisting or freezing, banking or custody disruption, smart-contract exploits, and regulatory action.

A stablecoin balance should not be assumed to be equivalent to risk-free cash.

## 21. Third-Party Protocol Risk

Onchain Matrix can interact with or depend on external protocols and infrastructure.

A failure in a third-party protocol can affect Onchain Matrix even if Onchain Matrix's own contracts operate as intended.

Third-party risks include exploits, governance attacks, oracle failures, insolvency, depegging, withdrawal restrictions, upgrades, liquidity loss, bridge failure, service outages, and changing terms.

## 22. Blockchain and Network Risk

BNB Chain and other supported networks can experience congestion, delayed transactions, high fees, validator or consensus failures, reorganizations, software bugs, RPC outages, forks, chain halts, or other disruptions.

Transactions can fail, be delayed, or execute at unexpected times or prices.

## 23. Wallet and Transaction Risk

Users can lose assets through lost private keys, compromised seed phrases, incorrect addresses, unsupported networks, malicious contract approvals, fraudulent interfaces, signing unintended transactions, or compromised devices.

Blockchain transactions are generally irreversible.

Always verify official contract addresses through the current Onchain Matrix GitBook, official Website, and the relevant block explorer before interacting.

Liquidity can disappear during stressed markets.

Selling can require accepting substantial slippage, a discount to estimated value, delayed settlement, partial execution, or no execution at all.

Liquidity assumptions used in models or examples can differ materially from real market conditions.

Laws, regulatory interpretations, enforcement priorities, tax treatment, sanctions requirements, licensing rules, or market-access restrictions can change.
