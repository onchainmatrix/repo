# Privacy Policy

**Last Updated: August 31, 2026**

This Privacy Policy explains how Onchain Matrix ("Onchain Matrix", "we", "us", or "our") can collect, use, disclose, retain, and protect information when you access or use the Onchain Matrix website, documentation, forms, interfaces, token-access features, dashboards, communications, and other services that link to this Privacy Policy (collectively, the "Services").

This Privacy Policy applies to information processed through the Services. It does not govern information independently processed by third-party websites, wallets, blockchains, exchanges, protocols, social platforms, or other services that maintain their own privacy policies.

## 1. Information We Can Collect

The information collected depends on how you interact with the Services.

### Contact and account-related information

We can collect information you provide directly, including:

* email address;
* information submitted through contact or inquiry forms;
* subscription preferences;
* verification codes and verification status;
* communications with Onchain Matrix; and
* other information you choose to provide.

### Wallet and blockchain information

When you connect a wallet or interact with blockchain functionality, we can process public wallet addresses, blockchain network information, transaction hashes, token balances or transaction history that is publicly available onchain, wallet connection status, smart-contract interactions, and other public blockchain data associated with an interaction.

Public blockchain information can be permanently visible and is not controlled by Onchain Matrix.

### Eligibility and compliance information

For restricted or permissioned features, including token-sale access, we can process information used to support eligibility controls, such as:

* country or jurisdiction confirmations;
* self-certifications and eligibility representations;
* wallet allowlist status;
* access-control results;
* IP address or IP-derived jurisdictional information;
* sanctions, fraud, abuse, or security screening results where applicable; and
* other information reasonably necessary to administer applicable participation restrictions.

The information required can differ by activity.

### Device, network, and usage information

When you use the Services, certain technical information can be collected automatically, including IP address, browser type, device type, operating system, language or regional settings, referring pages, pages or features accessed, timestamps, approximate location derived from IP address, diagnostic and performance data, and security or fraud-prevention signals.

### Cookies and similar technologies

The Services can use cookies, local storage, pixels, analytics technologies, or similar tools to operate website functionality, remember preferences, understand usage, measure performance, prevent fraud and abuse, and protect the security of the Services.

Browser settings can allow you to block or delete certain cookies. Disabling cookies can affect functionality.

## 2. Information We Do Not Request

## 4. Legal Bases for Processing

Where applicable data-protection law requires a legal basis, information can be processed on one or more of the following bases:

* **Consent** — where you choose to subscribe, provide optional information, or consent to a particular use.
* **Contract or pre-contractual steps** — where processing is necessary to provide a requested service or administer an activity governed by applicable terms.
* **Legitimate interests** — including securing the Services, preventing fraud, maintaining records, improving functionality, and protecting users and protocol infrastructure.
* **Legal obligations** — where processing is necessary to comply with applicable law, regulatory requirements, sanctions, court orders, or lawful government requests.
* **Protection of legal rights** — where processing is necessary to establish, exercise, or defend legal claims.

The applicable basis can depend on the jurisdiction and the specific processing activity.

## 6. Public Blockchain Data

Public blockchains are transparent systems.

Information written to a public blockchain can include wallet addresses, transaction amounts, token transfers, smart-contract interactions, transaction timestamps, and other transaction metadata.

Blockchain information can be visible to anyone and can be copied, analyzed, or associated with information from other sources.

Onchain Matrix cannot erase, modify, or guarantee the deletion of information recorded on a public blockchain.

Your data-protection rights therefore may not extend to modification or deletion of blockchain records that Onchain Matrix does not control.
