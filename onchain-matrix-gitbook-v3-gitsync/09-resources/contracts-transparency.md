# Contracts, Transparency & Public Verification

Onchain Matrix is designed so that important protocol activity can be verified through public onchain infrastructure wherever possible.

## Contract verification

Official documentation should maintain a current list of verified contracts for components such as:

- ONMX token,
- token-sale contracts,
- vesting or lock contracts,
- treasury or multisig addresses,
- future credit contracts,
- future pool and marketplace contracts.

**Do not rely on contract addresses copied from unofficial websites, social posts, direct messages, or search results.** Use only addresses published through official Onchain Matrix channels and verify them on the relevant block explorer before interacting.

## Treasury transparency

The transparency model should provide useful accountability without publishing operational detail that unnecessarily increases attack or market-manipulation risk.

Public reporting can include:

- treasury composition at an appropriate level of aggregation,
- approved allocation categories,
- treasury value and historical reporting,
- realized or reported yield,
- material treasury-policy changes,
- buyback and burn events,
- supply and vesting data,
- verified contract and multisig activity.

## What may remain operationally limited

For security and execution integrity, the protocol may avoid pre-announcing:

- exact future trade timing,
- signer identities or sensitive access details,
- internal credentials or infrastructure topology,
- transaction-level strategy instructions before execution,
- information that would create an obvious front-running or shorting target.

## Public accountability

Transparency is strongest when claims are linked to evidence. Published dashboards and reports should, where practical, reference onchain transactions, verified contracts, multisig activity, or other independently reviewable data.
