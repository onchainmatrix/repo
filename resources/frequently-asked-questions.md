# Frequently Asked Questions

<details>

<summary>What is Onchain Matrix?</summary>

Onchain Matrix is treasury-driven onchain capital infrastructure designed to connect protocol-owned treasury management, productive capital deployment, collateralized credit, tokenized debt markets, and ONMX-aligned protocol economics within one capital framework.

The protocol is being developed on BNB Chain.

</details>

<details>

<summary>What is the core purpose of the protocol?</summary>

The protocol is designed around four capital functions:

1. form capital,
2. preserve capital,
3. grow capital,
4. redeploy capital into productive infrastructure such as yield strategies, liquidity, credit, tokenized debt, and approved RWAs.

</details>

<details>

<summary>What is ONMX?</summary>

ONMX is the ecosystem alignment token of Onchain Matrix.

ONMX has a fixed maximum supply of **1,000,000,000 tokens** and is deployed on **BNB Chain**.

The official ONMX contract address is:

`0x0D60CC169b26be7fCcf0dEcB3B1Ee337fc5cbE5C`

Always confirm the current contract address through official Onchain Matrix channels before interacting with a token or smart contract.

</details>

<details>

<summary>Is ONMX backed by treasury assets?</summary>

No.

ONMX is not presented as a redemption claim on the protocol treasury. Holding ONMX does not mean that a holder owns a specific percentage of treasury assets or has a direct right to redeem treasury assets.

ONMX is designed as an ecosystem alignment token connected to protocol utility, treasury-driven value capture, supply discipline, and protocol participation where implemented.

</details>

<details>

<summary>What is the protocol-owned treasury?</summary>

The protocol-owned treasury is the productive capital base of Onchain Matrix.

Treasury capital is intended to be preserved, allocated, and accumulated over time rather than routinely consumed as operating cash.

Approved treasury capital can be allocated across stablecoins, major crypto assets, yield strategies, tokenized RWAs, liquidity, and future approved credit positions under defined risk controls.

</details>

<details>

<summary>Where does treasury yield come from?</summary>

Treasury yield is intended to come from productive capital deployment rather than ongoing ONMX emissions.

Potential sources can include:

* approved onchain yield strategies,
* major-asset strategies,
* tokenized RWA exposure,
* future approved credit participation,
* liquidity and other protocol activity.

Yield sources can change as market conditions, risk limits, liquidity, and strategy quality change.

</details>

<details>

<summary>Does Onchain Matrix mint ONMX to pay yield?</summary>

No ongoing ONMX emissions are intended to fund protocol yield.

ONMX has a fixed maximum supply of 1,000,000,000 tokens.

Treasury yield and protocol revenue are intended to come from deployed capital and protocol activity.

</details>

<details>

<summary>What is the 87.5% Core Treasury Allocation?</summary>

The **Core Treasury Allocation** combines token allocations associated with treasury formation and launch capital flows:

* 72.5% Treasury Reserve & LP
* 10.0% Public Sale
* 2.5% Presale
* 2.5% Seed

Together, these represent **87.5% of the maximum ONMX supply**.

The classification describes their intended role in treasury formation. It does not mean that 87.5% of ONMX is immediately circulating or simultaneously deployed.

</details>

<details>

<summary>Is the maximum supply the same as circulating supply?</summary>

No.

**Maximum supply**, **economically released supply**, **locked supply**, **treasury-controlled supply**, and **circulating supply** are different concepts.

The fixed 1 billion ONMX maximum supply is the supply ceiling. Tokens subject to locks, vesting, treasury controls, or other release conditions should not automatically be treated as public circulating supply.

</details>

<details>

<summary>What happens to the Treasury Reserve?</summary>

The Treasury Reserve is intended to remain subject to long-duration supply and treasury controls.

Final custody, vesting, lock, release, and deployment mechanics are governed by the applicable contracts, treasury policy, and public onchain records.

</details>

<details>

<summary>Are buybacks guaranteed?</summary>

No.

Surplus protocol revenue may support ONMX buybacks where permitted by protocol policy and where treasury, liquidity, market, legal, and operational conditions support the action.

A buyback should not impair treasury resilience and should not be understood as a guaranteed price-support mechanism.

</details>

<details>

<summary>What happens to ONMX that is repurchased?</summary>

Repurchased ONMX may be burned where the applicable protocol policy calls for supply reduction.

Completed burns should be independently verifiable onchain.

</details>

<details>

<summary>Are stablecoin distributions or airdrops guaranteed?</summary>

No.

Eligible stablecoin-based distributions or airdrops may be used where permitted and implemented.

Any distribution remains subject to legal requirements, participant eligibility, treasury conditions, protocol policy, and operational readiness.

ONMX is not designed around inflationary token distributions.

</details>

<details>

<summary>Is the treasury used to pay operating expenses?</summary>

The operating model is designed so that operations, infrastructure, security, liquidity support, and ecosystem growth can increasingly be funded from treasury-generated yield and protocol revenue rather than by routinely spending down the principal treasury base.

This is a design objective, not a guarantee that every future protocol expense will be covered by revenue in every period.

</details>

<details>

<summary>Is the treasury allocation fixed?</summary>

No.

Treasury allocations can change as market conditions, liquidity, risk, strategy quality, protocol needs, and treasury policy change.

Onchain Matrix uses an allocation framework rather than promising permanently fixed asset weights.

</details>

<details>

<summary>Does the protocol publish future treasury trades?</summary>

Not necessarily.

The protocol is designed to provide public accountability without pre-announcing operational information that could create front-running, shorting, security, or market-manipulation risk.

Executed activity and treasury reporting can be disclosed without publishing an exact future trade schedule.

</details>

<details>

<summary>Is the treasury performance guaranteed?</summary>

No.

Treasury strategies involve market, liquidity, smart-contract, oracle, counterparty, operational, stablecoin, and RWA risks.

Historical returns, treasury models, and projected scenarios are not guarantees of future performance.

</details>

<details>

<summary>Is the collateralized credit platform live?</summary>

The collateralized credit and tokenized debt layer is a roadmap component unless and until specific contracts and interfaces are publicly launched and verified.

The current protocol focus includes treasury formation, treasury infrastructure, ONMX, security controls, and launch-stage deployment.

</details>

<details>

<summary>How is the future credit layer designed to work?</summary>

The credit architecture is designed to support:

* P2P matching,
* pool-based liquidity,
* treasury participation under approved risk limits,
* crypto and tokenized RWA collateral,
* defined LTV and liquidation parameters,
* structured repayment and default terms,
* tokenized debt positions,
* approved secondary-market liquidity.

Final live parameters will be defined by the deployed contracts and applicable terms.

</details>

<details>

<summary>What is tokenized debt?</summary>

A tokenized debt position is an onchain representation of an approved credit position.

Where implemented, tokenization can support position transfer, fractionalization, accounting, and approved secondary-market liquidity.

Tokenized debt remains subject to the underlying credit, collateral, liquidity, legal, and smart-contract risks.

</details>

<details>

<summary>Who controls the treasury?</summary>

Critical treasury authority is designed around multi-signature control rather than unilateral access by one wallet or key.

Administrative permissions, upgrades, monitoring, and emergency controls are intended to use defined security and approval procedures.

Public documentation and onchain records should be used to verify the current control structure as it is deployed.

</details>

<details>

<summary>Can one person move the treasury?</summary>

The security architecture is designed to avoid unilateral treasury control.

Critical actions are intended to require multiple approvals under the applicable multisignature configuration.

</details>

<details>

<summary>Are protocol contracts audited?</summary>

External audit reports should only be treated as completed when the relevant report has been published through official Onchain Matrix documentation.

An audit can reduce certain risks but cannot eliminate smart-contract, operational, or economic risk.

</details>

<details>

<summary>How do I verify an official Onchain Matrix contract?</summary>

Use only addresses published through official Onchain Matrix channels.

Before interacting:

1. confirm the domain is `onchainmatrix.com`,
2. compare the address with the official GitBook deployment registry,
3. verify the network,
4. inspect the contract on the relevant block explorer,
5. never trust a contract address sent only through a direct message or unofficial social account.

</details>

<details>

<summary>What is the Seed Round?</summary>

The Seed Round is designed primarily to form the initial protocol-owned treasury base.

The current documented Seed structure includes:

* **Seed allocation:** 2.5% of maximum ONMX supply
* **Seed tokens:** 25,000,000 ONMX
* **Seed price:** $0.14 per ONMX
* **Public Sale / TGE reference price:** $0.20 per ONMX
* **Vesting:** 6-month cliff followed by 18-month linear vesting
* **Claims:** monthly after the cliff under the applicable vesting implementation
* **Primary purpose:** treasury formation
* **Seed capacity:** $3.5 million

</details>

<details>

<summary>Does the $0.20 reference price guarantee the market price at TGE?</summary>

No.

The $0.20 Public Sale / TGE reference price is a transaction term or target reference. It is not a guarantee of future market price, exchange liquidity, listing price, or token appreciation.

</details>

<details>

<summary>Are Seed tokens liquid at TGE?</summary>

Seed tokens are subject to the applicable vesting schedule.

The documented structure includes a 6-month cliff followed by 18 months of linear vesting, meaning Seed participants do not receive unrestricted immediate Seed liquidity at TGE.

</details>

<details>

<summary>Who can participate in Seed Access?</summary>

Seed participation is permissioned and subject to:

* eligibility,
* jurisdictional restrictions,
* wallet and access controls,
* applicable Terms,
* technical availability.

The live Seed interface and Terms control eligibility. Access to the interface does not by itself guarantee eligibility to participate.

</details>

<details>

<summary>What is used for Seed settlement?</summary>

The current public Seed overview identifies **USDT** as the settlement route.

The live Seed interface and applicable transaction terms control the supported network, payment asset configuration, and transaction process at the time of participation.

</details>

<details>

<summary>Where should I get official updates?</summary>

Use the official Onchain Matrix website and GitBook as the primary directory for current protocol information, contract addresses, documentation, and official community links.

Do not rely on unsolicited direct messages for token-sale instructions, wallet addresses, or contract addresses.

</details>
