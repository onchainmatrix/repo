# Capital Allocation & Yield Framework

Treasury capital is designed to be diversified across functional sleeves rather than managed as one undifferentiated pool.

## Stablecoin & liquidity base

This sleeve preserves maneuverability and supports:

- liquidity needs,
- credit funding capacity,
- market-response flexibility,
- reserve requirements,
- lower-volatility capital storage.

## Core crypto allocation

Approved major crypto assets can provide strategic market exposure and can support productive treasury strategies when the risk-adjusted opportunity is acceptable.

## Base yield strategies

Base strategies prioritize durability and capital protection. Selection criteria can include:

- protocol maturity,
- contract security,
- liquidity depth,
- operational history,
- withdrawal mechanics,
- concentration risk,
- yield sustainability.

## RWAs & approved credit

Tokenized RWAs and approved credit positions can diversify return sources when pricing, custody, liquidity, oracle, legal, and settlement risks are acceptable.

## Selective higher-yield opportunities

Higher-yield strategies can be considered only as capped sleeves within approved risk limits. Their purpose is not to maximize headline APY; it is to improve capital efficiency when risk-reward conditions are favorable.

## Strategy approval

A strategy can be rejected even when expected yield is attractive. Security risk, liquidity risk, smart-contract exposure, oracle design, or unfavorable exit conditions can outweigh return potential.

## No fixed public allocation promise

Treasury allocation is intended to remain responsive to market conditions. Public documentation should describe the framework and risk principles without implying that every asset weight or deployment amount is permanently fixed.
